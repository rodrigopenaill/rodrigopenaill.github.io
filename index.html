Long-Term Risk Differentiation

An earlier version circulated as my CEMFI master's thesis, “Permanent and Transitory Unobserved Heterogeneity in Credit Ratings” (2025).

November 2026

Paper Master's thesis (2025)
Temporary fluctuations in borrowers' financial conditions can change their credit ratings even when there are no changes in persistent creditworthiness. Under the Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) framework, calibrating probabilities of default to long-run experience does not correct rating assignments driven by these fluctuations. This paper quantifies differences between ratings based on current and persistent creditworthiness and examines whether incorporating the latter improves long-term default-risk differentiation. Using Bank of Spain credit scores for Spanish firms over 2000–2023, I estimate a state-space model separating persistent firm-specific creditworthiness from aggregate movements and transitory firm-specific variation. Smoothed estimates provide a benchmark, while filtered estimates yield ratings based on current and past scores. Point-in-time (PIT) ratings differ from the benchmark in 51.2% of firm-year observations, compared with 27.9% for filtered ratings. Filtered ratings increasingly outperform PIT ratings in differentiating future defaults as the horizon lengthens for medium, small, and micro firms, but not for large firms. Neither mechanical smoothing nor removing aggregate movements reproduces these gains, highlighting the importance of firm-specific transitory variation.

Unobservable No More: Estimating the Natural Rate of Interest under Flat IS and Phillips Curves

with Gabriele Fiorentini, Alessandro Galesi, Gabriel Pérez Quirós and Enrique Sentana

CEPR Discussion Paper DP21256

JEL E43, E52, C32, C52

Latest draft CEPR DP
We show that the Laubach and Williams (2003) model and its variants in Holston, Laubach and Williams (2017, 2023) cannot estimate the natural rate with finite precision when either the IS curve or the Phillips curve are flat. To solve this unobservability, we propose a simple augmented model with a mean-reverting interest rate gap that considerably narrows the natural rate's confidence bands in those empirically relevant situations. We also assess the ability of the corporate risk premium and the share of working age population to explain movements in the natural rate, but they generate filtered estimates that fluctuate too much.
